Young Sherlock on Prime Video review: fun, stylish, and better when you stop expecting classic Sherlock

By MovieJam Crew4 min readTags: Prime Video · Sherlock Holmes · Mystery Adventure

Official Amazon / Prime Video artwork used as the hero image.

Young Sherlock is easier to enjoy once you stop demanding the most faithful Holmes adaptation ever made. This is not built like a quiet, precision-first detective puzzle. It is built like a fast, stylish Prime Video adventure with Guy Ritchie fingerprints all over it: swagger, motion, attitude, and enough conspiracy energy to keep the binge moving.

That choice mostly works. Hero Fiennes Tiffin plays Sherlock as raw, impulsive, and still under construction rather than already-legendary. The result is a version of Holmes that feels much less sacred and much more accessible. Some viewers will love that. Some will think it sands down the magic. Both reactions make sense.

MyMovieJam verdict: this is a real yes with caveats. If you want a fun mystery-adventure binge with pace, style, and a younger Sherlock who actually feels young, start it. If you want top-tier detective brilliance or the cleanest season landing, go in with slightly lower expectations. Rating: 7.9/10.

Why it works

The biggest win is momentum. Young Sherlock rarely feels sleepy. It understands that origin stories die when they get too self-important, so it keeps shoving Sherlock into new danger, new suspicion, and new chaos. That makes the show much easier to watch than a more reverent version might have been.

The other thing that lands is the remix energy. The series is not pretending to be an untouched Arthur Conan Doyle artifact. It is openly playing in the Holmes sandbox with a younger, louder, more crowd-friendly setup. That looser approach also helps the supporting dynamics. The Moriarty relationship, in particular, gives the season extra life whenever the plot starts flirting with overcomplication.

What viewers are actually saying

  • The positive lane is clear: the audience conversation keeps circling the same words: fun, bingeable, and stylish.
  • The craft people notice first: pace, music, and the Guy Ritchie-flavored presentation come up a lot when viewers explain why they kept going.
  • The honest caveat: even people who liked it often admit the later stretch gets sillier, messier, or a little weaker than the start.

Watch if

You like youthful origin-story takes, energetic mystery-adventure shows, and Sherlock material that is happy to bend the canon for entertainment value.

Skip if

You want the sharpest detective logic, a colder more fully formed Holmes, or a prestige mystery that sticks every landing.

Audience pulse

The broad reaction feels aligned: people are enjoying the ride more than worshipping it. That is a perfectly healthy recommendation zone.

The honest caveat

Young Sherlock is not elite Holmes. It is also not trying very hard to be. The show is better at building a lively atmosphere than at delivering a flawless detective experience, and that gap matters if you came in hoping for one all-timer mystery reveal after another.

There are stretches where style covers for plot wobble. There are character beats that feel more “cool streaming-series move” than “this is exactly how Sherlock Holmes should evolve.” But that does not kill the recommendation. It simply narrows it. This is a smarter pick for viewers chasing fun and momentum than for viewers grading pure Holmes fidelity.

Bottom line

Young Sherlock works best as a crowd-friendly Sherlock remix with real pace and enough personality to justify the time. It does not beat the best Holmes adaptations at detective rigor. It does not need to. Its job is to be watchable, distinctive, and easy to recommend to the right Prime Video viewer. It clears that bar.

The independent MyMovieJam score is 7.9 / 10. That means we recommend it, but with the honest note that the fun is more consistent than the depth.
