Spider-Man (2002) review: still one of the easiest superhero yeses

Published · August 8, 2026 By MovieJam Crew 4 min read

Spider-Man still works because it understands one thing a lot of louder superhero movies lose: Peter Parker has to feel like a real person before the suit means anything. Tobey Maguire makes the awkwardness and decency land, Sam Raimi gives the whole thing comic-book momentum without losing the human scale, and Willem Dafoe goes exactly the right amount of gloriously unhinged as Green Goblin.

This is not the darkest Spider-Man movie, the funniest Spider-Man movie, or the most visually polished Spider-Man movie in 2026. It is still one of the clearest and most rewatchable. The origin beats are efficient, the emotional stakes are easy to track, and the movie never gets so busy trying to look important that it forgets to be fun.

MyMovieJam quick take: this is a 9.1/10 easy yes. If you want a superhero movie with heart, velocity, and a villain performance that knows exactly how big to play, Spider-Man (2002) still absolutely earns the rewatch.

Why it still lands

The cleanest win is sincerity. The movie believes in Peter's awkwardness, his guilt, and his basic goodness, so the transformation into Spider-Man feels earned instead of automatic. That matters more than perfect effects. You care because the story gives Peter actual vulnerability before it gives him crowd-pleasing power.

The second big win is tone control. Raimi keeps the movie bright, kinetic, and slightly pulpy, but there is enough emotional weight underneath Uncle Ben, the double-life pressure, and Norman Osborn's collapse to stop the whole thing from becoming empty theme-park spectacle. Dafoe especially understands the assignment. Green Goblin is theatrical, weird, and dangerous in a way that makes the movie more memorable every time he shows up.

Watch if

You want a heartfelt superhero origin, a very rewatchable Peter Parker, and a movie that still knows how to entertain without acting ashamed of comic-book sincerity.

Skip if

You need ultra-modern CGI, a darker adult-only register, or the rapid-fire joke style newer superhero movies lean on more heavily.

Best description

Earnest, clean, and still hugely watchable. Not the flashiest Spider-Man entry, but one of the easiest to love.

What keeps it from absolute perfection

Some of the effects obviously belong to an earlier era, and a few action beats now look more iconic than seamless. The Mary Jane material is also thinner than the Peter-and-Norman material, which means the emotional triangle never feels as sharp as the hero-villain conflict. Those are real limitations.

But the movie survives them because the storytelling stays clean. You always understand what Peter wants, what he fears, and what the next hit of pressure is. That kind of clarity ages well. Plenty of newer superhero movies have cleaner VFX and much foggier emotional logic. Spider-Man has the opposite problem, which is the better problem to have.

Audience-fit answer in one line: if you want a superhero movie that still feels human, funny, and crowd-pleasing without becoming weightless, this is one of the safest yes picks in the whole comic-book lane.

Bottom line

Spider-Man (2002) is still one of the cleanest “just watch it” recommendations in the genre. The emotional core is strong, Dafoe brings elite villain chaos, and the movie moves with enough speed and personality that the older effects stop mattering faster than you expect. MyMovieJam lands at a confident 9.1 / 10.
