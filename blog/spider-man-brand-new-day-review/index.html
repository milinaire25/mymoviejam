Spider-Man: Brand New Day review: a strong reset with just a little MCU drag

Published · August 8, 2026 By MovieJam Crew 4 min read

Official theatrical poster used as the hero image.

Spider-Man: Brand New Day works because it finally gives Tom Holland a cleaner, more grounded lane again. The movie understands that after all the cosmic noise and nostalgia fireworks, there is real value in letting Peter Parker feel smaller, lonelier, and more local. That reset helps almost immediately.

The strongest version of this film is the one built around Peter trying to operate with less safety net, less comfort, and more New York pressure. When Brand New Day stays in that street-level zone, it feels sharp, personal, and properly Spider-Man. The weaker version is when the movie starts reminding you it also has bigger franchise obligations to carry.

MyMovieJam quick take: this is an 8.8/10 strong yes. If you wanted a more back-to-basics Tom Holland Spider-Man movie with real character focus and cleaner hero pressure, Brand New Day delivers more often than it misses.

Why it works

The biggest win is focus. Holland gets more room to play Peter as a person again instead of just a relay point between larger MCU machinery. That makes the movie feel more emotionally stable even when the action gets louder. The best scenes are not just the spectacle scenes. They are the ones where Peter looks stretched, isolated, or forced to solve problems without easy support.

The second win is tonal clarity. The movie feels more like Spider-Man protecting a city and less like Spider-Man surviving an event calendar. That does not mean it becomes tiny or boring. It just means the stakes are easier to feel. The film remembers that Spider-Man is strongest when the audience can track the human cost under the mask, not just the crossover implications around it.

Watch if

You wanted Tom Holland back in a more classic, grounded Spider-Man lane with stronger Peter Parker material and cleaner New York energy.

Skip if

You are currently exhausted by MCU connective tissue and want a superhero movie with almost zero franchise setup hanging off it.

Best description

A good reset movie. More personal than overloaded, more emotionally useful than empty, and smarter when it stays close to Peter.

Where it stumbles

The honest caveat is that the movie does not stay perfectly disciplined. You can feel the bigger universe tugging at it in places, and some of that future-facing setup is more functional than exciting. It is not enough to sink the film, but it is enough to stop it from feeling completely free.

That is the gap between a very good Spider-Man movie and a truly top-tier one. Brand New Day gets a lot right, especially with tone and Peter's point of view, but it occasionally hands attention away from its strongest material. The good news is that the strongest material is strong enough to win anyway.

Audience-fit answer in one line: if you wanted Holland's Spider-Man to feel more grounded and emotionally centered again after the bigger multiverse era, this is one of the clearer yes recommendations in the current superhero lane.

Bottom line

Spider-Man: Brand New Day is not flawless, but it is exactly the kind of correction this corner of Marvel needed. Tom Holland is better served by the narrower character focus, the street-level energy gives the film a healthier pulse, and the movie mostly avoids drowning its core in franchise noise. MyMovieJam lands at a confident 8.8 / 10.
