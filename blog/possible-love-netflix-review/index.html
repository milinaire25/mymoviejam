Possible Love on Netflix review: viewers call Lee Chang-dong's return a masterpiece, if you can sit with it

MyMovieJam scoreRating card →8.6/10Great
By Milind Patil5 min readNetflix · Korean Drama · Audience First

Possible Love is Lee Chang-dong's first feature since Burning. Two married couples cross paths while making a documentary and end up staring at class, desire, and what they want from each other's lives. It premiered at Venice on September 6, 2026, and arrived on Netflix on November 6. It runs close to three hours.

Most people who finished it are loud about how good it is. A smaller group is honest about the length.

MyMovieJam verdict: if you want a patient, novelistic Korean drama and you can sit with a nearly three-hour runtime, Possible Love is a strong 8.6 / 10. The performances are the draw. If you need a light Friday binge, this is not it.

What viewers are actually saying

We read the reactions on X and Reddit before scoring this. Festival posts and early Netflix chatter land in the same place: the writing and the cast are the reasons to watch, and the runtime is the reason some people hesitate.

  • @whitecitycinema on X: “Lee Chang-dong's POSSIBLE LOVE is a masterpiece, and the best new movie I've seen all year.” Post
  • @nikgroz on X: “What an incredible writer-director Lee Chang-dong is; follows up the sensational Burning with another novelistic masterpiece” Post
  • r/LondonFilmFestival: “I loooved this film so much, it was so meticulously written… it didn’t even feel like three hours actually… amazing performances all around” Thread
  • r/Koreanfilm: “With all that said, the film might as well be worth watching purely for the elite cast. Absolutely phenomenal acting all around I can't praise or love them enough!” Thread

That last Reddit post is useful because it is not pure praise. The writer still lands on the cast as reason enough to watch. That matches the broader pulse: craft love is durable, accessibility complaints are real but narrower.

Watch if

You want a patient, novelistic Korean drama with award-caliber performances and don't mind a nearly three-hour slow burn.

Skip if

You need plot propulsion, short runtime, or a light Friday binge — this is deliberate and demanding.

Audience pulse

Warm on craft and performances. The complaints are about length and pace, not about the acting or the writing.

Why it works

Lee Chang-dong builds the film like a novel. Scenes sit. People talk past each other. The documentary-within-the-story setup lets class and desire show up without a speech about them. Viewers who liked Burning keep saying this feels like the same writer-director working at full strength again.

The cast is what people single out when they are not ready to call the whole film perfect. Even mixed posts land on the performances. If you care about that kind of acting more than plot momentum, this is the title for you this week.

The honest caveat

It is long. Close to three hours. Some festival viewers say it did not feel that long. Others will feel every minute if they came for plot propulsion. This is deliberate and demanding. It is not a background watch.

If your bar for a Friday night is something under two hours with a clear hook in the first ten minutes, skip it. The people who love this film are the ones willing to sit still for it.

Bottom line

If you want one serious Korean drama this week and you already know Lee Chang-dong's pace works for you, start with Possible Love. Most viewers who finish it rate the writing and the cast high. The ones who bounce mostly wanted a shorter, faster movie.

The independent MyMovieJam score is 8.6 / 10. We recommend it, with a clear warning about the runtime.
